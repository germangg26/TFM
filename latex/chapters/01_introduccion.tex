\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Context and motivation}

Email survives the proliferation of advertising channels for an economic reason: its cost per impact is low and it allows the message to be tailored to each recipient's profile. That advantage, however, only materialises when the company gets right who to contact and with which product. A campaign sent indiscriminately spends the same budget as a targeted one, yet yields far less; the problem, therefore, is not sending emails, but deciding well who to address each one to.

In practice, campaign managers face an investment-allocation problem under incomplete information: the user's actual behaviour towards an email is hard to anticipate, and the available data tend to be scarce, noisy and heavily imbalanced between positive and negative interactions. The actual click rate in email marketing campaigns is usually below 5\%, which turns behaviour prediction into a classification problem with highly imbalanced classes.

Machine learning offers tools to address this problem systematically, combining signals from past behaviour with features of the user and of the advertised product. Nevertheless, its effective application requires careful methodological decisions, especially regarding the treatment of class imbalance, the scarcity of observed interactions and the incorporation of external contextual information.

\section{Research question and objectives}

The central question of this work is the following: \textit{to what extent is it possible to predict the probability of a user interacting with an email marketing campaign from their demographic profile, their behavioural history and the features of the advertised product, and what type of signal proves most decisive?}

To answer this question, the following objectives are set out:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Build a preprocessing pipeline that integrates campaign data with geographic and demographic information from external sources, generating complete and homogeneous user profiles.
    \item Develop two levels of unsupervised segmentation: a purely demographic segmentation for assigning new users (cold start), and a behavioural segmentation based on the interaction history.
    \item Implement a propensity model that combines user and product features ---including the email subject text---, comparing algorithms and architectures (flat versus hierarchical), validated without data leakage and with explicit correction of the sampling bias introduced by balanced training.
    \item Build a product recommender system and evaluate it in a controlled manner (temporal split, reference baselines and statistical testing), determining which signal (behavioural or demographic) proves most useful for ranking.
    \item Predict the weekly click volume through time-series modelling to support campaign planning.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Approach and contribution.} The emphasis of this work is methodological. Rather than maximising an accuracy metric, it builds a reproducible end-to-end pipeline (from raw data to an example deployment) that takes each decision in a justified manner: validation without data leakage, comparison of each model against trivial baselines before adopting it, correction of the sampling bias and statistical testing of every claim. The contribution is not a top-performing predictor, but a process that determines, with evidence, which signal is useful, which model deserves to be deployed and at what cost. For this reason, several of the following hypotheses are posed precisely so that they can be \emph{refuted}: the value of the work lies as much in confirming them as in discarding them with sound grounds. In sum, this Master's dissertation illustrates how a data team would approach a project aimed at maximising the ROI of campaigns: what to build at each stage, how to validate it and how to decide what deserves to be deployed and what does not. The object of study is, therefore, \emph{the method} as much as the models.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Hypotheses.} From the question and the objectives, the work tests four falsifiable hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1.} The \emph{product} features (sector, category and subject text) significantly improve click propensity over a model that uses only user features and over the popularity baseline.
    \item \textbf{H2.} The enriched demographic features add predictive power to click propensity; this is tested despite their synthetic nature (Chapter~\ref{cap:datos}), precisely in order to measure how much individual signal they retain.
    \item \textbf{H3.} A recommender based on the user's behavioural segment outperforms the popularity baseline in product ranking.
    \item \textbf{H4.} The Prophet model improves the forecast of click volume over naïve baselines (naïve and moving average).
\end{itemize}
The testing of each hypothesis is addressed, respectively, in modules M2 (Chapter~\ref{cap:propension}; H1 and H2, the latter via the ablation study), M3 (Chapter~\ref{cap:recomendador}; H3) and M4 (Chapter~\ref{cap:forecast}; H4).

\section{Structure of the dissertation}

The dissertation is organised following the flow of the pipeline. Chapter~\ref{cap:estado_arte} reviews the state of the art in propensity modelling, customer segmentation, recommender systems and time-series prediction applied to digital marketing. Chapter~\ref{cap:datos} describes the data sources, the cleaning process, the demographic-geographic enrichment and the exploratory analysis. Chapter~\ref{cap:evaluacion} provides an overview of the pipeline and the common evaluation framework (metrics, leakage-free validation and statistical tests). Next, each module is presented in its own chapter, setting out its method and its results together: customer segmentation (Chapter~\ref{cap:segmentacion}), propensity model (Chapter~\ref{cap:propension}), recommender (Chapter~\ref{cap:recomendador}) and time-series forecasting (Chapter~\ref{cap:forecast}). Chapter~\ref{cap:negocio} covers the example deployment and the return-on-investment analysis. Finally, Chapter~\ref{cap:conclusiones} gathers the discussion, the conclusions and the lines of future work.
